Then he stood on ladders, steps and chairs, and used a brush and a bucket of whitewash. At last he had dust in his throat and eyes, white splashes all over his black fur, an aching back and tired arms. Spring was moving in the air above him and in the earth below and around him. It came even into his dark, low little house, and brought its special feeling of not being happy with what he had, and of wanting more. So it was not strange that he suddenly threw his brush down on the floor. He said, 'Bother!' and 'O blow!' and 'Hang spring-cleaning!' He ran out of the house without waiting to put on his coat. Something above was calling him in a strong way, as if it must be obeyed. He went to the steep little tunnel. For him it was the same as the gravel road in front of the house for animals whose homes are nearer the sun and air. He scraped and scratched and scrabbled and scrooged, and then did it all again in another order. He worked busily with his little paws and said to himself, 'Up we go! Up we go!' At last, pop! his nose came out into the sunlight, and he found himself rolling in the warm grass of a great meadow.
