turnover|A small pie made by folding food inside a flat piece of dough.
infirm|Weak or broken. Here Beth uses it for her old, hurt toys.

evolutions|Planned movements. Here Laurie makes funny movements behind his old relative to make Jo laugh.
puckered|Pulled into small lines, like cloth that is pulled tight.

bridled|Lifted the head and showed anger or pride because of something said.
pacify|To make an angry person quiet and happy again.
crosspatch|A person who is often cross and in a bad way.

dishevelled|Not tidy. It is used for hair or clothes that are not in good order.

frizzled|Made into small, tight rings of hair.
plantation|A large place where one kind of plant is grown. Here Jo plans a big bed of tall yellow flower.
mignonette|A garden plant with small flower that has a sweet smell.
larkspur|A tall garden plant with blue, pink or white flower.
bower|A cool place in a garden, under plants that grow over a stick frame.
earwiggy|Full of earwigs. Earwigs are small insects with two sharp parts at the back.
troubadour|A man who sang love songs and played music long ago.
saleratus|A white powder used when you make bread and cake, so that they get big and light.
nosegay|A small group of flower that has a sweet smell.

wherry|A light, small boat that people move with long sticks.

lackadaisical|Showing a weak, dream-like way of acting, as if too tired or too sad to care.

bandbox|A round box made of thin board, used to keep a hat. Here Jo makes a joke about it.
bedewed|Made wet with small drops. Here it means wet with tears.
treadmill|A big wheel that people or animals walk on and never get anywhere. Here Meg uses it for days that never change.
spatted|Hit with the flat hand, quickly and many times. Here Amy hits and shapes her clay.
ninepence|A small amount of money, worth nine old English coins.
mandarin|A toy of a Chinese man whose head moves up and down. Here Beth moves her head like one.
didoes|Silly jokes and games. Here Hannah uses it for the way Laurie plays.
pewmonia|Hannah's way of saying 'pneumonia', a very bad sickness of the lungs.
pendulum|The part of a clock that goes from side to side and keeps it going.
belladonna|A medicine made from a plant that can kill. At that time people took it to try to stop a bad sickness with red skin.
camphor|A white thing with a strong smell, used as medicine.
parched|Very dry, because you are very hot or have not had a drink.
wan|Looking tired and not well, with a white face.
pallor|A very white face, from illness or fear.
electrified|Gave someone a sudden, strong surprise.
affianced|Promised to be married; engaged.
turban|A long cloth that is wound round the head like a hat.
rosary|A string of round beads that some people use when they say their prayers.
insinuatingly|In a clever, soft way, not saying things straight, to get what you want.
stampede|A sudden run of many people at once, in great excitement.
blemish|A small mark that makes something look worse.
jungfrau|A German word for a young girl. Here it is the name Jo and Laurie give to the girl they make of snow.
philander|To play at love in a light, silly way. Here Jo says it is fun to watch other people do it.
perversity|A wish to do the opposite of what people want you to do.
abject|Very low and humble, showing no pride.
hazed|Played rough or unkind games on new students at college.
pinafore|A loose cover with no arms, worn over a dress to keep it clean.
whatnot|A light piece of furniture with open shelves for small things.
moire|A cloth made of silk that looks like waves of water.
salver|A flat tray, often made of silver, for carrying glasses or food.
cravat|A cloth that a man wears round his neck, like a tie.
lurid|Very bright in a strange, shocking way.
afflatus|A strong rush of ideas that seems to come from a higher power.
tatting|A kind of fine cloth with holes in a pattern, made by hand.
ruthlessly|With no feeling of pity.
bounteous|Very generous; more than enough.
pungent|Having a strong, sharp smell or taste.
furbelows|Extra pieces of cloth on a dress, for show.
mantua|A woman's loose dress. A 'mantua-maker' is a woman who makes dresses.
organdie|A very thin, hard cloth, used for summer dresses.
puritanical|Too strict about what is right and wrong, and against fun.
akimbo|With hands on the hips and elbows pointing out.
illuminated|Painted with bright colors and gold, like the big letters in old books.
magnanimous|Very generous and kind, especially to an enemy.
hansom|A light carriage for hire with two wheels, pulled by one horse. The driver sits up behind.
barouche|A fine open carriage with four wheels, and a top that folds back.
colliery|A place where men dig coal from under the ground.
gallows|A wood frame on which bad people were killed with a rope.
bootblack|A person, often a boy, who cleans people's shoes for money.
latticed|Having crossed strips, as in an old window.
biddies|Hens. The word can also mean fussy old women.
woodbine|A plant that climbs, with flower that has a sweet smell. Here it is a wild plant with red leaves.
reverie|A state of thinking about nice things, like a dream.
horsehair|Hair from a horse, used to fill and cover chairs.
insinuating|Trying in a clever, soft way to make someone like you.
slipshod|Careless and not in good order, as in speech.
meerschaum|A pipe for smoking, made of a soft, white stone.
hyacinth|A spring flower with a thick stem and many small flower that has a sweet smell. It grows from a round root.
standish|A stand that holds pens and ink on a table.
bashful|Not at ease and easily upset in front of people.

lucre|Money, as something low. 'Filthy lucre' is a joke name for money.

marplot|A person who gets in the way and spoils a plan.
homilies|Talks about right and wrong, like short talks in church.
lapping|The soft sound of small waves on the shore.
equipages|Fine carriages with their horses and the men who work with them.
flounces|Wide extra pieces of cloth on the bottom of a woman's skirt.
piquante|Lively and a little bold, in a nice way.

fender|A low metal guard in front of a fire, to stop pieces of burning wood falling out.
delinquencies|Small faults in doing one's duty.
perjured|Having broken a promise that was made as a serious oath to God.
millinery|Women's hats and making them.

panniers|Large baskets that hang on each side of a donkey or horse to carry things.

indolence|A dislike of work; being lazy.

strand|The shore of a sea or a river.
